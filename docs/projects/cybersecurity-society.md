## Why this is on a technical portfolio

Because the technical explanation of a breach is usually the least interesting
part of it. The vulnerability is the mechanism. The reason it stayed
unpatched, or the reason nobody escalated the alert, is the actual cause. This
coursework is about that second half.

## Equifax breach analysis

Research into the 2017 Equifax breach. The initial vector was a known
vulnerability in Apache Struts with a patch available before the intrusion.
The technical finding is a single sentence, and it explains almost nothing on
its own.

What is worth studying is the organizational failure around it. How a patch
with a published advisory went unapplied, how the intrusion went undetected
for as long as it did, and what that says about the difference between having
a vulnerability management process and having one that works.

problems are structural rather than individual. Devices ship with default
credentials, have no practical update mechanism, stay deployed for a decade,

## Human factors

Coursework applying behavioral models to security, including work applying
Maslow's hierarchy of needs to how people relate to technology, and
criminological theory covering strain theories, deterrence research, and
biological explanations of offending.

The connection to the day job is direct. Deterrence research in particular
tells you something uncomfortable about security controls, which is that the
certainty of being caught changes behavior far more than the severity of the
consequence. That has implications for how you design monitoring, and it
argues against a lot of common policy.
